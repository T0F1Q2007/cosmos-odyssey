## 1. The Photosphere & Convective Granulation

### 1.1 Physical Structure & Temperature Stratification
The solar photosphere constitutes the optical surface of the Sun (optical depth $\tau_{500} \approx 1$). It is a thin boundary layer approximately $400\text{ km}$ thick, operating at an effective blackbody temperature:
$$T_{\text{eff}} = 5,778 \pm 3\text{ K}$$
At the base of the photosphere, the temperature is approximately $6,400\text{ K}$, falling to the solar temperature minimum of approximately $4,300 - 4,400\text{ K}$ at the boundary with the chromosphere ($z \approx 500\text{ km}$).

### 1.2 Convective Granulation Mechanics (DKIST / Rimmele et al. 2020)
* **Spatial Scale:** Individual granules possess characteristic diameters of $1,000 - 1,600\text{ km}$ ($1.3'' - 2.2''$ angular resolution from Earth).
* **Convective Thermodynamics:**
  - **Granule Cores:** Buoyant, hot, ionized plasma rises at vertical velocities of $1.5 - 2.5\text{ km/s}$. The core temperature reaches $6,000 - 6,200\text{ K}$, appearing bright yellow-white.
  - **Intergranular Lanes:** As plasma radiates energy into space, it cools by $\Delta T \approx 400 - 800\text{ K}$ (dropping to $5,200 - 5,400\text{ K}$). This cooled, dense plasma sinks along narrow, polygonal, jagged lanes under negative buoyancy at velocities reaching supersonic downflows of $3.5 - 7.0\text{ km/s}$.
  - **Cellular Geometry:** Granules form dynamic Voronoi-like convex polygons with an average of 5 to 6 sides. Granule lifespans range from 8 to 12 minutes before exploding or dissolving.
* **Supergranulation:** Large-scale convective patterns spanning $30,000 - 35,000\text{ km}$ across, with horizontal flow velocities of $300 - 500\text{ m/s}$ that sweep magnetic flux tubes to the network boundaries, forming intense magnetic filigree and bright faculae ($\Delta I/I \approx +10\text{ to } +20\%$).

## 2. Sunspots & Active Regions (Hale's Law & Penumbral Dynamics)

### 2.1 Magnetic Field Topology & Temperature Contrast
Sunspots are manifestations of strong magnetic flux tubes ($B \approx 2,500 - 4,000\text{ Gauss}$) emerging through the photosphere from the solar tachocline ($r \approx 0.7 R_\odot$). The extreme magnetic pressure ($B^2 / 8\pi$) strongly inhibits convective heat transport.
* **Central Umbra:**
  - Temperature: $T_{\text{umbra}} \approx 3,700 - 4,200\text{ K}$ (appearing deep charcoal/ruby in visual comparison to the surrounding $5,800\text{ K}$ photosphere).
  - Magnetic orientation: Nearly vertical ($\theta_B < 15^\circ$).
  - Fine structure: Peppered with tiny "umbral dots" ($\sim 150 - 300\text{ km}$) where localized convective plumes penetrate.
* **Surrounding Penumbra:**
  - Temperature: $T_{\text{penumbra}} \approx 5,400\text{ K}$.
  - Magnetic orientation: Inclined, fanning out horizontally ($40^\circ \le \theta_B \le 75^\circ$).
  - Filamentary Structure: Composed of thousands of radially oriented bright and dark convective rolls/filaments.
  - **Evershed Flow:** Systematic radial outflow of gas along penumbral filaments at horizontal speeds of $2 - 6\text{ km/s}$.
* **Bipolar Pairs & Hale's Polarity Law:** Active regions almost invariably form bipolar magnetic pairs aligned roughly parallel to the solar equator (with Joy's tilt law: leading spot closer to the equator). The leading spot is typically more compact and symmetrical, while the following spot is more fragmented.

## 3. The Chromosphere & Spicules

### 3.1 The Dynamic Chromosphere ($T \approx 10,000 - 25,000\text{ K}$)
The chromosphere extends $2,000 - 3,000\text{ km}$ above the photosphere. Key spectral lines include:
* $\text{H}\alpha$ (Hydrogen-alpha, $\lambda = 656.28\text{ nm}$): Brilliant scarlet/crimson.
* $\text{Ca II H \& K}$ ($\lambda = 396.8\text{ nm}, 393.4\text{ nm}$): Deep violet.
* $\text{He II}$ ($\lambda = 30.4\text{ nm}$, SDO/AIA 304): Incandescent orange-red extreme UV.

### 3.2 Spicule Forest at the Solar Limb
* **Type I Spicules:** Driven by acoustic/shock waves leaking through the photosphere; reach heights of $6,000 - 9,000\text{ km}$, velocities of $20 - 40\text{ km/s}$, lifespans of $5 - 12\text{ minutes}$.
* **Type II Spicules (DKIST / De Pontieu et al.):** Fast, violently heated plasma jets driven by magnetic reconnection; velocities of $50 - 150\text{ km/s}$, lifespans of $10 - 100\text{ seconds}$, heated to transition region temperatures ($> 100,000\text{ K}$).
* **Visual Appearance:** The solar limb is NOT a smooth geometrical sphere. It possesses a dynamic "burning prairie grass" or "forest" of over 300,000 simultaneous spicules tilting along local magnetic field vectors.

## 4. Solar Flares, Flare Ribbons & Moreton Waves

### 4.1 Magnetic Reconnection & Energy Partition
When magnetic shear and twist in coronal active regions exceed stability thresholds, magnetic reconnection occurs, converting stored magnetic energy into:
1. Thermal plasma heating ($T > 10 - 30\text{ million K}$).
2. Kinetic particle acceleration (electrons and protons accelerated to relativistic velocities).
3. Radiation across the entire electromagnetic spectrum (radio to hard X-rays and gamma-rays).

### 4.2 Visual Flare Phenomena
* **Impulsive Phase:** Blinding white-hot hard X-ray / white-light kernel flashes directly at the magnetic footpoints.
* **Flare Ribbons:** Two parallel or conjugate glowing ribbon tracks running along the magnetic inversion line, separating at $10 - 50\text{ km/s}$ as reconnection proceeds higher into the corona.
* **Moreton Waves (Chromospheric Shockwaves):** Fast-mode magnetosonic shock waves propagating radially outward across the chromosphere at $500 - 1,500\text{ km/s}$. As the wave sweeps across the solar disk, it compresses the chromosphere, causing visible expanding ripple rings in $\text{H}\alpha$ and SDO/AIA EUV channels (coronal EIT waves).

## 5. Coronal Loops, Prominences & Filaments

### 5.1 Coronal Loops
* Closed magnetic flux tubes arching above active regions, rooted in opposite-polarity sunspots.
* Temperature: $1.5 - 4\text{ million K}$ in quiescent loops; $> 10\text{ million K}$ during flare heating.
* Visual appearance in SDO AIA 171 ($\text{Fe IX}$, $0.6\text{ MK}$, golden-yellow) and 193 ($\text{Fe XII}$, $1.2\text{ MK}$, bronze-gold): graceful, multi-strand magnetic arcades with glowing plasma flowing along the magnetic vectors.

### 5.2 Prominences (Limb) vs. Filaments (Disk)
* Cold, dense chromospheric plasma ($T \approx 7,000 - 10,000\text{ K}$, density $100\times$ higher than surrounding corona) suspended stably in magnetic dips for weeks (quiescent) or hours (eruptive).
* **On the Solar Disk:** Viewed against the brilliant $5,800\text{ K}$ photosphere, they absorb light and appear as dark, sinuous, snake-like ribbons called **filaments**.
* **On the Solar Limb:** Viewed against the darkness of outer space, they emit brilliant $\text{H}\alpha$ and $\text{He II}$ radiation, appearing as magnificent arching loops, trees, and curtains called **prominences**.

---

## 6. Coronal Mass Ejections (CMEs)

* Giant bubbles and flux ropes of magnetized plasma ejected into the heliosphere at speeds from $250\text{ km/s}$ to $> 3,000\text{ km/s}$.
* **Classic Three-Part Morphology (SOHO/LASCO & STEREO):**
  1. Leading bright compressed shock front (density enhancement).
  2. Dark magnetic cavity (the twisted magnetic flux rope / magnetic void).
  3. Bright, dense, ragged core (the erupting prominence filament).

## 7. WebGL & Three.js Physical Shader Implementation

### 7.1 Physical Limb Darkening (Eddington-Barbier Approximation)
The apparent surface brightness of the Sun drops toward the limb because line-of-sight optical depth probes cooler, higher atmospheric layers:
$$\frac{I(\mu)}{I(1)} = 1 - u(1 - \mu) - v(1 - \mu)^2$$
Where $\mu = \cos\theta = \sqrt{1 - (r/R)^2}$ ($r$ is radial screen distance, $R$ is solar radius). For visual light ($\sim 550\text{ nm}$):
$$u \approx 0.64, \quad v \approx 0.18$$
* **Center ($\mu = 1$):** Blinding, radiant yellow-white core ($T \approx 6,400\text{ K}$, color: `vec3(1.0, 0.98, 0.92)`).
* **Limb ($\mu \to 0$):** Deep, warm amber-crimson rim ($T \approx 4,400\text{ K}$, color: `vec3(0.92, 0.38, 0.04)`).

### 7.2 Voronoi / Cellular Convective Granulation
Instead of smooth FBM noise (which looks like synthetic clouds), real granulation must be modeled using 2D/3D Voronoi / Worley cellular noise:
* Granule center ($F_1$ distance is large): high intensity, bright yellow-white upwelling.
* Intergranular lane ($F_1 \to 0$ near boundaries): narrow, dark crevices, non-linear edge sharpening via `pow(edge, 4.0)`.
* Domain warping with curl/simplex noise produces the fluid, boiling convective churning observed by DKIST.

### 7.3 Realistic Magnetic Prominences & Corona
* **No Blocky Particles:** Untextured square point primitives must NEVER be used.
* **Coronal Loops:** Continuous parametric 3D cubic Bézier spline tubes with additive alpha and flowing plasma textures along the tube axis.
* **Spicule Fringe:** A noisy vertex-displaced or raymarched shell with radial spikes along the limb.
* **Multi-Layer Corona:**
  1. K-Corona (Thomson electron scattering): dense, white-hot, intense radial falloff $I \sim r^{-6}$.
  2. E-Corona / Streamers (Emission lines): delicate, organic helmet streamers radiating along solar dipole lines out to $3 - 5 R_\odot$.
